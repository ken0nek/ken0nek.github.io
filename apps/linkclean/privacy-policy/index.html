LinkClean

Privacy Policy
Last updated: September 14, 2026

LinkClean ("LinkClean", "we", "us") removes tracking parameters from links before you share them. We built it to be private by default: there is no account to create, we run no server that sees your links, and the links you clean are processed on your device. This policy explains what the app does — and the little it sends off your device.

What we collect

No personal information, and never your full links. LinkClean has no user accounts and asks for no name, email, or contact details. The links you clean are processed on your device, and we never receive the full link. The only data about you or your links that the app sends off your device on its own is anonymous, aggregate usage analytics, described next. As part of that, the app includes the website domain of a cleaned link (for example, youtube.com) so we can understand which sites people use LinkClean on, along with non-identifying summary information about the tracking parameters it found — never the full link, and never anything that identifies you.

Analytics

To understand which features people use and where the app can improve, LinkClean sends anonymous, aggregate signals — for example, that a link was cleaned or that a screen was opened — to one or more privacy-focused analytics providers, chosen because they are designed not to identify you personally. These providers may process and store those signals outside your own country, including in the European Union and the United States.

These signals are tied only to a random, per-install identifier that is not linked to your name, email, or Apple ID. Beyond counts and basic interactions, what they take from a link is limited to its website domain (for example, youtube.com), plus non-identifying summary information about the tracking parameters found in it — such as how many there were and what kind.

About parameter names. A parameter name is sent only when it already appears on LinkClean's own built-in tracker lists, which ship publicly inside the app. Any other name — including any rule you add yourself — never leaves your device through these signals. LinkClean works out a general category for it on your device and sends only that.

These signals never include your clipboard, your search terms, the full link, its path, or any parameter values. This version of LinkClean does not include an in-app setting to turn analytics off.

What stays on your device

  • Your history. Each link you clean can be saved to a history list kept in private storage on your device. It is shared between LinkClean and its share extensions on your device, but it never leaves your device and is not synced to iCloud or anywhere else. Turning off "Save history" stops new entries from being saved; clearing history — or deleting the app — removes them.
  • Your settings and custom parameters. Your preferences (such as auto-paste and save-history) and any custom tracking parameters you add are stored locally on your device.
  • Clipboard, only when you enable auto-paste. If you turn on auto-paste, LinkClean reads a link from your clipboard when you open or return to the app, so it can offer to clean it. This happens on your device; your clipboard contents are not sent anywhere.

When LinkClean reaches the internet

Cleaning happens on your device. LinkClean still makes ordinary web requests — to the websites your links point to, so it can show you a title or a thumbnail; to our own site, for configuration that keeps the app current; and to Apple, for App Store information — alongside the anonymous analytics described above. None of these sends your links to a server of ours, and none of them carries an account or anything that identifies you.

Like any web request, each reveals your device's IP address and standard connection information to the service that answers it, under that service's own privacy policy. A request to a website carries only the link itself, and never your clipboard, your history, or your settings.

Things you choose to send

Beyond what is described above, LinkClean sends nothing on its own. Some features let you send us something deliberately. Whenever that happens, LinkClean shows you exactly what will be sent before it leaves your device, and sends it only when you say so.

Nothing you send this way carries an account or a device identifier, or ties what you send to anything else you've sent, and LinkClean never adds a full link, its path, or any parameter values to it.

What we do not collect or do

  • No account, name, email, or phone number.
  • No location data.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No cross-app or cross-site tracking, and no App Tracking Transparency prompt — there is nothing to track.
  • No selling of your data, and no sharing it with advertisers or data brokers.
  • The full links you clean are never sent to us or included in analytics — only a cleaned link's website domain (for example, youtube.com) and non-identifying summary information about the tracking parameters found in it, as anonymous analytics described above.

Children's privacy

LinkClean is a general-audience utility and is not directed to children under 13. It has no accounts and collects no personal information, so it collects none from children. The anonymous usage analytics described above contain no personal information from anyone.

Your rights

LinkClean has no user accounts and stores no name, email, or contact information, so there is no profile for us to access, correct, or delete — we hold nothing that identifies you. The links and data you create live on your device, and deleting the app removes them. The anonymous analytics we receive are aggregate and not linked to you, so they cannot be traced back to an individual to retrieve or erase. If you reside in a region with privacy laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, you have the rights those laws provide; you are welcome to contact us with any question.

Changes

If we make material changes to this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page.
